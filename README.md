## Rhythmic Pattern Transfer in Electronic Dance Music
### Background and Motivation
Electronic Dance Music (EDM) production relies heavily on precise rhythmic structures, yet recent research has revealed that groove perception depends on more than temporal positioning alone. Brøvig-Hanssen et al. (2021) demonstrate that EDM producers working within grid-based timing aesthetics achieve expressive variation primarily through sound manipulation—amplitude envelopes, attack shaping, and volume automation—rather than microtiming deviations. This finding is reinforced by Danielsen et al. (2023), whose cross-cultural study on musical microrhythm established that perceived timing depends fundamentally on sonic characteristics: attack time, duration, and spectral content all influence when listeners perceive an event to occur, with sounds having "beat bins" rather than precise temporal locations.
### Existing Approaches to Music Generation and Style Transfer
Recent work in symbolic music generation has demonstrated the effectiveness of discrete representations for learning musical patterns. Han et al. (2020, 2021) applied VQ-VAE to symbolic loop generation, extracting 8-bar MIDI patterns from the Lakh MIDI Dataset and learning discrete latent codes representing common rhythmic frameworks. Their approach achieved superior fidelity and diversity compared to MuseGAN and Music Transformer baselines, demonstrating that discrete representations effectively capture pattern-to-pattern structures in music. However, their work operates entirely in the symbolic domain and focuses on generation rather than transformation.
Style transfer in music has primarily addressed complete musical transformation. Brunner et al. (2018) introduced MIDI-VAE for neural style transfer on symbolic music, successfully transforming pieces from Classical to Jazz styles by learning disentangled latent spaces encoding both content and style. While pioneering, this approach transfers harmonic and melodic characteristics alongside rhythm, and requires symbolic input throughout. Similarly, Li (2022) developed LooPy, a framework for rendering professional EDM tracks from symbolic representations with sophisticated mixing and effects processing, but this system generates from scratch rather than transforming existing audio.
### The Gap: Rhythmic Framework Transfer for Audio
Despite advances in both symbolic generation and complete style transfer, no existing work learns rhythmic frameworks from one genre and applies them to transform arbitrary audio while preserving original timbral content. Bruford's (2021) work on drum loop retrieval developed sophisticated similarity metrics and pattern analysis tools for symbolic representations, but focused on classification and retrieval rather than generative transformation. Gómez Marín's (2018) statistical modeling of EDM drum styles explored pattern discovery but did not address cross-domain application or audio synthesis.
The technical challenge lies in bridging symbolic pattern learning with audio-domain transformation. While pattern learning benefits from the discrete, fixed-length representations that VQ-VAE handles effectively (Han et al., 2021), the sonic characteristics that define EDM groove—as identified by Brøvig-Hanssen et al. (2021) and Danielsen et al. (2023)—exist in the continuous audio domain.
### Proposed Approach and Extensions
This work addresses this gap through a hybrid pipeline: (1) train VQ-VAE on EDM rhythmic patterns represented as quantized grids, (2) decompose arbitrary input audio into sound segments via onset detection, (3) cluster segments using spectral similarity to map to learned pattern categories, (4) encode and decode through trained VQ-VAE to obtain EDM-style rhythmic positioning, and (5) reconstruct audio by placing original sound segments at new positions.
The current proof-of-concept uses binary grid representations (note present/absent), following Han et al.'s approach. However, the research by Brøvig-Hanssen et al. and Danielsen et al. indicates this discards critical information. Planned extensions include:
#### Velocity/dynamics integration: 
Expanding the grid representation from binary to continuous values encoding RMS amplitude at each onset. This captures the "intensity dynamics" that Brøvig-Hanssen et al. identified as crucial for EDM groove, where volume automation creates rhythmic "breathing" even on-grid.
#### Attack envelope characteristics: 
Adding attack time as an additional feature dimension. Danielsen et al.'s finding that attack shape fundamentally affects perceived timing suggests that preserving or learning attack patterns will improve both the perceptual quality and the "EDM-ness" of transferred patterns.
These extensions maintain the discrete pattern learning approach that proved effective in Han et al.'s work while incorporating the continuous sonic parameters that recent perceptual research identifies as essential for groove. The result would be a system that learns not just "when kicks and snares land" but "how they land"—the temporal framework combined with dynamic and timbral characteristics that define EDM rhythmic identity.
This approach differs from pure generation (which requires learning audio synthesis) and complete style transfer (which transforms all musical dimensions). Instead, it isolates rhythmic framework as the transfer target while preserving the user's source material, enabling applications in remixing, sketch development, and cross-genre experimentation where preserving original sound character is desirable.
